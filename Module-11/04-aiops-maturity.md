# 04 — AIOps Maturity Model

Most organisations do not jump from firefighting to autonomous self-healing overnight. They move through recognisable stages, and the tools, processes, and cultural changes at each stage are distinct. The **AIOps Maturity Model** gives you a map for where you are now, what the next stage looks like, and what specifically you need to build to get there.

---

## The Four Levels

```text
Level 1: Reactive    → "We find out from users that something is broken."
Level 2: Proactive   → "We alert before users notice."
Level 3: Predictive  → "We prevent failures hours before they happen."
Level 4: Autonomous  → "The system heals itself. Humans review, not respond."
```

Most engineering teams today operate at Level 1–2. Elite teams (Netflix, Google SRE, Spotify) operate at Level 3–4 for their most critical services.

---

## Level 1 — Reactive

**Definition**: The team discovers problems from user complaints, support tickets, or social media — not from internal monitoring.

**Characteristics**:
- Monitoring exists but is incomplete or alert-heavy (alert fatigue)
- MTTR is measured in hours to days
- Post-mortems are rare or blame-focused
- On-call is a punishment, not a rotation

**Capabilities present**:
- Basic uptime monitoring (ping checks)
- Some log access
- Manual dashboards

**What Modules 1–3 taught you**: Setting up Prometheus, Grafana, and structured logging — the foundation of moving out of Level 1.

| Metric | Level 1 Benchmark |
|---|---|
| MTTR | 2–12 hours |
| TTD | Users report first |
| Alert noise | > 80% of alerts are noise |
| Post-mortem rate | < 20% of major incidents |

---

## Level 2 — Proactive

**Definition**: The team detects problems before users do, using automated alerts with meaningful thresholds.

**Characteristics**:
- Prometheus + Alertmanager with tuned alert rules
- SLOs defined; error budgets tracked
- On-call rotation with runbooks for common alerts
- Post-mortems written for every SEV-1 and SEV-2

**Capabilities present**:
- Metric-based alerting (CPU, disk, error rate, latency)
- Log aggregation with search
- Basic anomaly detection (static thresholds)
- Runbooks for common failures

**What Modules 4–7 taught you**: Structured log parsing, anomaly detection, SLOs, and Alertmanager — the core of Level 2 operations.

| Metric | Level 2 Benchmark |
|---|---|
| MTTR | 30 minutes – 2 hours |
| TTD | < 5 minutes (from metric alert) |
| Alert noise | 40–60% actionable |
| Post-mortem rate | > 80% of major incidents |

---

## Level 3 — Predictive

**Definition**: The team uses historical data and ML models to predict failures hours or days before they occur and acts proactively to prevent them.

**Characteristics**:
- Time-series forecasting for capacity planning
- Risk scoring across multiple dimensions
- Predictive alerts that fire before a threshold is crossed
- DORA metrics tracked; deployment quality improving

**Capabilities present**:
- Linear regression / ARIMA forecasting (Module 9)
- Composite risk scoring
- Error budget burn rate forecasting
- Chaos engineering (Module 10) to verify resilience

**What Modules 8–10 taught you**: Log correlation, incident root cause analysis, predictive maintenance, auto-remediation — the core of Level 3 operations.

| Metric | Level 3 Benchmark |
|---|---|
| MTTR | < 30 minutes (with guided runbooks) |
| TTD | Predictive alert fires 2–24h before failure |
| Alert noise | < 20% noise |
| Prevented incidents | > 50% of potential incidents avoided |

---

## Level 4 — Autonomous

**Definition**: The system detects, diagnoses, and remediates a large class of known failures without human intervention. Humans review what happened, improve the playbooks, and handle novel failures.

**Characteristics**:
- Auto-remediation for known failure patterns (Module 10)
- LLM-generated RCA and post-mortem drafts (Module 11)
- Feature flag auto-rollback on anomaly detection
- Chaos engineering is continuous, not quarterly
- Human-in-the-loop only for novel, high-risk scenarios

**Capabilities present**:
- Ansible/Terraform auto-remediation
- GitOps for infrastructure and flag state
- Local LLM for incident analysis
- Automated post-mortem draft generation

**What Module 11 taught you**: LLM-based incident analysis, automated RCA, and the full capstone end-to-end — the entry point to Level 4.

| Metric | Level 4 Benchmark |
|---|---|
| MTTR (for known failures) | < 5 minutes (automated) |
| Human-paged incidents | < 30% of total incidents |
| Post-mortem draft | Auto-generated by LLM within 60 seconds |
| Change failure rate | < 5% (elite DORA) |

## Key Takeaway

Maturity is not a destination — it is a direction. No team maintains Level 4 across all services simultaneously. Netflix runs chaos engineering constantly but still has incidents they couldn't predict. Google SRE with 15 years of investment still has Level 1 problems in newly launched products.

The goal is to move your most critical, highest-impact services toward Level 3–4 first, learn what works in your environment, then propagate those patterns across the organisation.
